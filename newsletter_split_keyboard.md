##  Hey,

Remember when split keyboards were a fringe obsession? Soldering irons, exposed microcontrollers, a cult of columnar-layout zealots who insisted your wrists would thank them in seven to ten business years?

That era is officially over.

This week, three trends hit at once — and together, they tell one unmistakable story: **the split keyboard niche just graduated from hobby to product category.** Below: what changed, why now, and the three plays that matter for anyone building, investing,, or buying in this space.

Let's get into it.

##  The Hook: Dygma Broke Its Own Religion

In August, **Dygma** — a brand that spent its lifetime preaching the gospel of fully-split ergonomic keyboards — quietly detonated its own doctrine.

They launched the **Raise 2**: a board whose two halves magnetically *click together* into a single, unified unit. For ergonomics, you split it. For a café, a flight, or a hotel desk, you snap it shut and throw it in your bag.

The marketing team titled the announcement **"We BROKE the Golden Rule of Ergonomic Keyboards."** The post hit #1 on r/DygmaLab. The implication is seismic: *the most dogmatic brand in the category just admitted compromise sells.*

That single decision is the leading indicator for everything else happening this week.

---

##  The Three Trends Defining the Week

### **1. The Modular "Splittable But Not Split" Form Factor**
*The flagship reframe.*

Dygma isn't alone in this thinking — they're just the loudest. The hybrid work era broke the dedicated ergonomic setup. Workers now move between laptops, shared offices, and home desks in the same week. A board that *forces* you into split mode is friction. A board that *offers* split mode is flexibility.

**Strategic significance:** High. Dygma's first-mover window is **6–12 months** before Logitech, Keychron, or Razer clones magnetic-split architecture. Watch the late-2026 product cycle for OEM responses.

 *Source:* [Dygma — "We BROKE the Golden Rule"](https://dygma.com/blogs/product-development/we-broke-the-golden-rule-of-ergonomic-keyboards) | [r/DygmaLab thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/DygmaLab/)

---

### **2. Wireless Trackball Splits Hit the Mainstream**
*The category-defining innovation.*

**BastardKB's Charybdis line** — Dactyl-Manuform splits with palm-positioned trackballs — just crossed the line from open-source kit to **prebuilt, wireless, ready-to-ship product**. The Nano, Mini, and MK2 are now purchasable, Bluetooth-enabled, and showing up on AliExpress, Etsy, and Reddit comparison threads.

Why this matters: the trackball-split collapses **two ergonomic-correctness movements into one device.** You're not just solving keyboard strain — you're solving *mouse* strain simultaneously. The whole desktop input layer becomes a single, untethered unit.

The timing is perfect:
- Bluetooth LE + ZMK firmware finally deliver weeks-long battery life.
- RSI awareness has peaked post-COVID — knowledge workers with home-office injuries are actively shopping.
- The trackball renaissance (Kensington, Logitech MX Ergo) has created a parallel audience ready to consolidate.

**Strategic significance:** **Highest of the three.** This is the 3–5 year category-defining bet. If a major OEM enters this space with a polished product, the addressable market expands by 10–50x. BastardKB and Holykeebs are small operations with real scaling challenges — capital is the bottleneck, not demand.

 *Source:* [BastardKB Charybdis landing page](https://bastardkb.com/charybdis/) | [r/ErgoMechKeyboards — Charybdis Nano vs Keyball39](https://www.reddit.com/r/ErgoMechKeyboards/comments/1qbgh62/charybdis_nano_or_keyball39/)

### **3. Budget "Gateway" Splits Crash the Price Floor**
*The volume unlock.*

**EPOMAKER's Split70** — sub-$100, row-staggered, wireless, hot-swap, gasket-mount — is the first mass-market budget split from a brand with real distribution muscle. It's generating dedicated review threads on xbitlabs, hlplanet, shkspr.mobi, and retrohandhelds within weeks of launch.

Simultaneously, there's a parallel surge in **low-profile split ergo** interest — the Nomad and Nyx open-source projects, plus a growing "Best Low-Profile SPLIT Ergo keyboards?" thread that's attracting newcomers who don't want the visual bulk of a "medical device" on their desk.

This trend demolishes all three historical barriers to entry:
- **Price:** $99 vs. the old $200–$400 floor.
- **Learning curve:** Row-staggered (no columnar relearning required).
- **Aesthetic intimidation:** Looks like a normal keyboard that happens to split.

**Strategic significance:** **Highest near-term volume impact.** Expect Keychron, Royal Kludge, and likely Logitech or Razer to release sub-$100 splits within 12 months. *Caveat:* There's real churn risk — buggy budget boards can poison newcomers against the entire category. Watch QA reviews closely.

 *Source:* [EPOMAKER Split70](https://epomaker.com/products/epomaker-split70) | [Justin's Beginner's Guide to Split Keyboards (Feb 2026)](https://justinmklam.com/posts/2026/02/beginners-guide-split-keyboards/)

##  The Big Picture: A Three-Pronged Mainstreaming

These aren't competing narratives. They're **sequential stages of the same lifecycle**:

| Stage | Trend | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Open the funnel** | #3 Budget Gateway | Remove price/learning barriers |
| **Define the ceiling** | #2 Wireless Trackball | Show what premium becomes |
| **Bridge the two** | #1 Modular Hybrid | Serve both camps |

The split keyboard niche is simultaneously becoming **more flexible** (Trend 1), **more integrated** (Trend 2), and **more affordable** (Trend 3). That's the signature of a category exiting enthusiast adolescence.

---

##  Honorable Mention — The Quiet Enabler: ZMK Studio

While you're watching the hardware, watch the firmware. **ZMK Studio** — a GUI tool that lets users reconfigure wireless split keymaps at runtime without reflashing — is rapidly becoming the default firmware story for 2026. It's the unsung infrastructure that makes wireless beginner-friendly splits *actually* beginner-friendly. Battery life claims of **660+ hours** on ZMK boards are now routine.

If you're building wireless splits in 2026, ZMK Studio support isn't optional.

 *Source:* [ZMK Studio docs](https://zmk.dev/docs/features/studio) | [QMK vs ZMK comparison](https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/qmk-vs-zmk-wireless-firmware-comparison)

##  What to Do With This

**️ If you're a hardware startup:**
The trackball-split (Trend #2) is your highest-upside bet. Low competition, defensible IP, system-level innovation that can't be cloned with a simple form-factor tweak. Raise now — the window is open.

** If you're at an incumbent (Logitech, Keychron, Razer):**
The budget split (Trend #3) is your immediate volume play. Your product cycles are 12 months. Move.

** If you're a premium brand (Dygma, ZSA, Kinesis):**
The modular hybrid (Trend #1) is your existential reframe. Without flexibility, you get squeezed between budget boards at the low end and integrated systems at the high end. *Flexibility is your moat.*

** If you're a buyer:**
The category has never been more accessible. $99 gets you in the door. $350 gets you the flagship. $400+ gets you the future (trackball-split). Pick your tier — they're all legitimate now.
