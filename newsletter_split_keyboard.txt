## The Hook

**The keyboard you've been typing on is about to become extinct.**

Not literally. But this week, three separate trends converged on the same conclusion: **the split keyboard just escaped the hobbyist basement and walked into Best Buy.**

For the better part of a decade, "ergonomic split" meant hand-wired PCBs, Discord group buys, and three-month Reddit rabbit holes. That era is ending — fast. A $300 ROG gaming split with magnetic switches shipped to retail. A 420-gram aluminum split you can stuff in a jacket pocket is going live on Kickstarter. And the trackball-on-keyboard crowd finally has a one-stop shop.

This is the week the niche became a category. Here's what just happened — and what it means for your next purchase.

---

##  The Three Trends That Matter This Week

###  TREND 1 — Hall-Effect Splits Go Mainstream for Gaming
**The Signal:** ASUS — yes, *that* ASUS — shipped the **ROG Falcata**, a wireless 75% split with magnetic Hall-effect switches and 8 kHz polling. It's the first time a major OEM has married "split ergo" with "competitive gaming" in a real, retail product.

**Why it matters:** Until now, the gaming world had magnetic switches (Wooting, SteelSeries, Keychron) and the ergo world had splits — and the two universes never met. The Falcata broke that barrier. Wooting's own subreddit is now debating whether to build a split. That's competitive urgency.

**The catch:** Ergo purists are grumbling that the Falcata isn't *truly* ergonomic — not enough tenting, not enough column stagger. There's a buyer-mismatch risk: gamers buy it, never learn touch typing on columns, and return to a TKL within 18 months.

**Impact Score: 8.5/10**

---

### ️ TREND 2 — The Split That Fits in Your Bag
**The Signal:** Two ultraportable splits are making noise. The **ElimKeys Elytra** (11.8 mm thick, 420 g, full aluminum, Kailh Choc V2, Bluetooth + USB-C) hits Kickstarter December 16. The **JOMAA Portable Folding Split** folds to 55 mm and ships on Amazon now.

**Why it matters:** For the first time, splits are being marketed to *people who have never heard of QMK*. Knowledge workers, frequent flyers, MacBook users who would never build a Corne but would spend $200 on something that fits in a backpack and doesn't wreck their wrists.

**The math:** Even0.5% of the portable keyboard market =500k+ units/year. That's10–25x the current commercial split market.

**The catch:** Crowdfunding execution risk. ElimKeys is young. JOMAA is a generic Amazon listing with unclear support. The trend is sound even if specific products flame out.

**Impact Score: 7.5/10**

---

### ️ TREND 3 — Splits That Kill the Mouse
**The Signal:** Charybdis, Keyball, NAYA, and the Holykeebs ecosystem are all gaining traction. Reddit threads this week are asking *"What's an affordable split with a trackball?"* and the answers all converge on the same handful of designs. BastardKB's Charybdis Nano is even spawning AliExpress clones — a positive demand signal.

**Why it matters:** Reaching for a mouse is the single largest contributor to non-ergonomic posture. Boards that integrate a trackball, trackpoint, or touchpad eliminate that reach entirely. Holykeebs is now positioning as the canonical one-stop-shop — trackball, trackpoint, and touchpad under one roof.

**The catch:** This trend has been "almost breaking out" for five years. The learning curve is the highest of the three. The aesthetic is a commitment. There's no Falcata-style catalyst forcing mainstream adoption.

**Impact Score: 6.0/10**

---

##  The Meta-Signal

What makes this week unusual isn't any single trend — it's that **all three are firing simultaneously.** The split keyboard form factor is exiting its hobbyist phase and entering a productization phase. For years, this category moved one trend at a time. Now it's moving on three vectors at once: *performance gaming, mobility, and input consolidation.*

That's a category-maturation pattern. Think mechanical keyboards circa 2015–2017, when suddenly multiple form factors and switch types all matured at once.

---

##  What To Do Now

**If you're a gamer who cares about wrist health:**
 Look at the ROG Falcata. It's the first product that doesn't force you to choose between competitive edge and ergonomic form factor. Real-world reviews are positive but note the ergonomics are a compromise — not a purist split.

**If you travel and type a lot:**
 Watch the ElimKeys Elytra Kickstarter (Dec 16 launch). 420g, aluminum, low-profile choc switches — this is the first portable split that doesn't feel like a typing downgrade. The JOMAA foldable is on Amazon now if you need something today.

**If you're tired of reaching for a mouse:**
 Holykeebs is your starting point. Charybdis Nano is the most accessible entry. Just know the learning curve is real — budget two weeks before judging.

**If you're still on a traditional keyboard:**
 Pick one trend. The portable split (Trend 2) has the lowest barrier to entry for non-enthusiasts. That's where the category is heading next.
